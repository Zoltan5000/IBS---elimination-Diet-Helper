    Ordering tips that work almost anywhere

    • Choose plain grilled, roasted or steamed meat, fish, eggs or tofu, with rice, potato or polenta.
    • Ask for sauces, dressings and gravies on the side. They're the most common hidden source of garlic and onion.
    • Ask whether the kitchen can cook your dish in plain oil or butter, without garlic or onion.
    • Hard cheeses such as parmesan and cheddar are low in lactose. Cream sauces and soft cheeses aren't.
    • This checker is a guide only. Recipes vary, so ask staff when in doubt.

    Learn

    This is education, not medical advice. IBS should be diagnosed by a clinician, and guidelines recommend doing this diet with a FODMAP-trained registered dietitian. Talk to your doctor before you start, especially if you are pregnant, have diabetes, are underweight, or have a history of disordered eating.

    Your journey at a glance

    The guide, step by step

    1Before you start: get checked

    The low FODMAP diet is meant for people who already have an IBS diagnosis. Other conditions can cause the same symptoms, so a clinician should rule them out first.[NICE] [ACG]

    See a doctor promptly, before any diet change, if you have:

    • Blood in your stool or rectal bleeding
    • Weight loss you can't explain
    • Iron-deficiency anemia (low iron on a blood test)
    • A family history of bowel or ovarian cancer, inflammatory bowel disease or celiac disease
    • Symptoms that start for the first time later in life, or a lasting change to looser or more frequent stools

    NICE calls these "red flag" signs and ACG calls them "alarm features". They call for medical investigation, not a diet experiment.[NICE] [ACG]

    Read more: NICE CG61 recommendations ↗ ACG IBS guideline (Am J Gastroenterol, 2021) ↗

    Get tested for celiac disease before cutting wheat

    The low FODMAP diet reduces wheat, rye and barley. Celiac blood tests and biopsies depend on you eating gluten, so going low-gluten first can give a false negative. The ACG guideline recommends celiac testing in people with IBS-type diarrhea symptoms.[ACG] [Celiac]

    Read more: ACG celiac disease guideline (2023) ↗ ACG IBS guideline (Am J Gastroenterol, 2021) ↗

    Line up support

    • A FODMAP-trained dietitian. The British Society of Gastroenterology says the diet should be supervised by a trained dietitian, and NICE says this kind of diet advice should only come from a healthcare professional with dietary expertise.[BSG] [NICE]
    • Free patient guides from university hospitals, such as Michigan Medicine's MyGINutrition ↗, with food lists, recipes, label-reading tips and a tracking worksheet.[Michigan Medicine]
    • The Monash University FODMAP Diet app. It has lab-tested serving sizes and is updated as foods are retested.[Monash] [Monash]
    • A plan for the next 2–3 months. Avoid starting right before travel, holidays or major life events.

    Is this diet right for you?

    Guidelines rate the evidence for the low FODMAP diet as low quality but still recommend a limited trial, because it helps many people.[ACG] In the UK, first-line diet advice is offered to everyone first: regular meals, less tea and coffee, and cutting down on alcohol and fizzy drinks. If that hasn't helped, the low FODMAP diet is the usual second-line step.[BSG] [NICE]

    If you've had an eating disorder, or food rules make you anxious, talk to your clinician before starting. The BSG notes that the diet needs close monitoring to avoid nutritional gaps or overly restrictive eating, and recommends screening for this risk before starting.[BSG]

    Read more: BSG IBS guideline (Gut, 2021) ↗ NICE CG61 recommendations ↗

    2Understand what you're testing

    FODMAP stands for Fermentable Oligosaccharides, Disaccharides, Monosaccharides And Polyols. These are short-chain carbohydrates that are poorly absorbed in the small intestine. They draw water into the gut and are fermented by bacteria in the colon, which produces gas. In people with IBS, whose gut is often more sensitive, this can cause bloating, pain, diarrhea or constipation.[Monash]

    It works for many people: in a controlled trial at Monash University, people with IBS had lower overall symptom scores on a low FODMAP diet than on a typical Australian diet.[Halmos 2014]

    The diet tests six groups one at a time. People don't usually react to all of them. In a University of Michigan reintroduction trial, people reacted to an average of 2 FODMAP groups each, and fructans and GOS were the most likely to worsen pain or bloating.[Michigan]

    Read more: Monash: about FODMAPs and IBS ↗ Monash University trial (Gastroenterology, 2014) ↗ University of Michigan reintroduction trial (Clin Gastroenterol Hepatol, 2025) ↗

    What the research says, beyond Monash

    The diet started at Monash, but universities around the world have tested it independently:

    • King's College London (UK), 2017: in a blinded trial against a "sham" (fake) diet, 61% of people who completed the low FODMAP diet reported adequate symptom relief, compared with 39% on the sham diet.[Staudacher 2017]
    • University of Michigan (US), 2016: in IBS with diarrhea, low FODMAP reduced pain, bloating and urgency more than standard diet advice, although the main combined result didn't differ. 40–50% of people got adequate relief on either diet.[Eswaran 2016]
    • University of Gothenburg (Sweden), 2015: low FODMAP and traditional IBS diet advice helped about equally. After 4 weeks, 50% and 46% of people had responded.[Böhn 2015]
    • University of Sheffield (UK), 2022: traditional advice, low FODMAP and gluten-free diets worked about equally in IBS without constipation, and traditional advice was the easiest and cheapest to follow.[Rej 2022]
    • University of Leeds (UK), 2022: a pooled analysis of randomized trials ranked low FODMAP first among the diets studied for overall symptoms, pain and bloating.[Black 2022]

    The bottom line: the low FODMAP diet consistently does better than a usual or sham diet. Simpler traditional diet advice works about as well for many people, though, which is why UK guidelines suggest starting there.[BSG]

    What's changed over the years

    Advice on IBS and diet has moved on. These ideas have been dropped or overturned by current guidelines:

    • "Low FODMAP is a lifelong diet." Wrong. The strict phase is short, and the goal is to bring back as many foods as you can.[Monash] [AGA]
    • "IgG food intolerance tests show what to cut out." Not recommended for IBS (strong recommendation).[BSG]
    • "Go gluten-free for IBS." The BSG and Canadian guidelines don't recommend it, and say any benefit may come from eating less fructan in wheat rather than from removing gluten. NIH (NIDDK) patient information still lists avoiding gluten as something a doctor may suggest, so guidance differs.[BSG] [CAG] [NIDDK]
    • "Take probiotics for IBS." The ACG suggested probiotics in 2018 but reversed this in 2021, suggesting against them for overall symptoms. NICE (2008) says people who choose to try one should take it for at least 4 weeks and watch the effect.[ACG] [NICE]
    • "Low FODMAP beats every other diet." Tempered. In head-to-head trials, simpler traditional advice worked about as well for many people.[Böhn 2015] [Rej 2022]

    What's new: researchers are testing gentler versions that cut only the biggest FODMAP sources instead of all of them. Monash calls this "FODMAP gentle". It looks promising but hasn't been fully tested yet, so ask your dietitian whether it suits you.[Monash]

    3Track a baseline week

    Before changing anything, record what you eat and how you feel for about a week. Note your symptoms and when they happen. This baseline lets you tell later whether the diet actually helped.

    How to score

    Rate each symptom from 0 (none) to 10 (worst imaginable) once a day, at the same time. Note stool form too: the Bristol Stool Form Scale runs from type 1 (hard lumps) to type 7 (watery); types 3 and 4 are generally seen as the ideal range.[Bristol]

    Also log things that affect IBS but aren't food: stress, sleep, your menstrual cycle, exercise, and medications.

    Use the Log tab. Entries are tagged with your current phase automatically.

    4Phase 1: Low FODMAP (2–6 weeks)

    Swap high-FODMAP foods for low-FODMAP alternatives in the same food group, for example an orange instead of an apple. The aim is substitution, not cutting out whole food groups, so your diet stays nutritious.[Monash] Monash recommends 2 to 6 weeks for this phase.[Monash]

    2 weeks or 6 weeks?

    Sources give slightly different numbers, but they agree on the shape: stay strict for at least about 2 weeks, check whether it's helping, and stop by 6 weeks either way.

    • Monash University: 2–6 weeks[Monash]
    • American Gastroenterological Association (2022): no more than 4–6 weeks, with a set end date so the restriction isn't open-ended[AGA]
    • Michigan Medicine's clinic (2018): about 2 weeks[Michigan Medicine]

    The app lets you check in from 2 weeks and reminds you to stop by 6 weeks.

    Read more: AGA expert review on diet in IBS ↗ Monash: the 3 steps ↗ Michigan Medicine ↗

    Read more: Monash: the 3 steps of the FODMAP diet ↗

    Do

    • Plan meals and shop with a list for the first week
    • Check labels for honey, inulin, chicory root, high-fructose corn syrup, onion and garlic powder, and sweeteners ending in "-itol"
    • Use garlic-infused oil: fructans don't dissolve in oil, so it carries the flavor without the FODMAPs[Monash]
    • Keep fresh fruit to about 3 portions a day, spread out[NICE]
    • Keep up fiber with low-FODMAP sources such as chia seeds, flax seeds, quinoa, canned lentils, brown rice, oats and oat bran[AHS]
    • Keep logging symptoms daily

    Avoid

    • Staying in this phase longer than needed. The elimination phase isn't nutritionally complete, long-term restriction makes eating harder than it needs to be, and in one trial 4 weeks of restriction lowered bifidobacteria, a type of beneficial gut bacteria. As one Michigan Medicine gastroenterologist puts it, it "can change the gut flora in ways that we don't completely understand."[Michigan Medicine] [Staudacher 2012]
    • Cutting out foods that aren't high in FODMAPs "just in case"
    • Expecting perfection. A slip-up isn't a failure; note it and carry on.

    A sample low FODMAP day

    • Breakfast: Oats with lactose-free milk, blueberries and a teaspoon of maple syrup
    • Lunch: Rice or quinoa bowl with chicken or firm tofu, spinach, carrot, cucumber and an olive oil and lemon dressing
    • Snack: An orange and a small handful of walnuts
    • Dinner: Salmon with potatoes, green beans and zucchini cooked in garlic-infused oil
    5Check-in: did it help?

    Compare your recent diary scores with your baseline. The app compares them for you on the Today screen when you reach the check-in.

    Symptoms clearly better

    FODMAPs are likely playing a role. Move on to Phase 2 to find out which ones. Don't stay on the strict diet: reintroduction is where you learn what you can eat.

    Little or no change after 4–6 weeks

    FODMAPs may not be your main trigger. Return to your normal diet and talk to your doctor or dietitian about other options. Guidelines also support soluble fiber such as psyllium, medicines targeted to your IBS type, and gut-directed psychological therapies such as CBT or gut-directed hypnotherapy.[ACG]

    6Phase 2: Reintroduce one group at a time

    Stay on your low FODMAP base diet while you test one FODMAP group at a time, using a food that mainly contains that one FODMAP, such as mango for fructose.[Monash] Monash estimates this phase takes about 6 to 12 weeks.[Monash]

    The 3-day challenge

    1. Day 1: Eat a moderate serve of the test food.
    2. Day 2: If you feel fine, eat a larger serve.
    3. Day 3: If still fine, eat a large serve, about what you'd normally eat.
    4. Then a break: Go back to strict low FODMAP for a few days, until any symptoms settle, before the next test. The app gives you 3 days.

    Monash counts a challenge as passed if you had no symptoms, or only minor ones that didn't affect your day. A little wind and bloating is normal. If you get moderate or severe symptoms on any day, stop that challenge, don't move to the next day, and wait until you're back to baseline before testing the next group.[Monash]

    Fructans are often tested separately for grains, garlic and onion, because people react differently to each. Monash names mango as a good fructose test, because fructose is its main FODMAP. The other foods are common examples. Monash revised its challenge foods and serving sizes in 2025, so confirm your test foods and amounts with your dietitian.[Monash]

    Tips for a clean test

    • Test when life is relatively calm. Stress, illness, poor sleep or your period can cause symptoms that have nothing to do with the food.
    • Don't combine test foods with other high-FODMAP foods.
    • If a result is unclear, repeat that challenge later.
    • A reaction is about amount, not all-or-nothing. Note the largest serve you tolerated.

    The app walks you through each challenge day by day on the Today screen.

    7Phase 3: Your long-term diet

    The goal of the whole process is the least restrictive diet that keeps your symptoms under control. You only limit the groups, and amounts, that you reacted to, and bring everything else back.[Monash]

    • Tolerated groups: Eat these freely again.
    • Partly tolerated: Eat them in the amounts that were fine for you, and spread them out over the day.
    • Not tolerated: Limit them for now, but retest every few months. Tolerance often changes over time.

    Your personal results are on the Today screen once you reach this phase.

    Other habits that help

    • Eat regular meals and take your time. Try not to skip meals or leave long gaps between them.[NICE]
    • Choose soluble fiber, such as psyllium (ispaghula), and avoid insoluble fiber like wheat bran, which may make symptoms worse. Start low, around 3–4 g a day, and build up gradually.[BSG] [ACG] [NIDDK]
    • Limit tea and coffee to 3 cups a day, and cut down on alcohol and fizzy drinks.[NICE]
    • Manage stress, sleep and activity. The gut-brain connection is a real part of IBS, and gut-directed psychological therapies are recommended in guidelines.[ACG]

    Diet guidelines at a glance

    The key rules from the guides above in one place, each with its source. Numbers in brackets link to the full reference at the bottom of the page.

    1. Diagnosis first

    Get an IBS diagnosis and celiac testing while still eating gluten, and check for red flag symptoms before changing your diet.[ACG] [NICE] [Celiac]

    Read more ↗

    2. Use expert help

    The low FODMAP diet should be supervised by a trained dietitian, and foods should be reintroduced according to your own tolerance.[BSG] [NICE]

    Read more ↗

    3. Start with the basics

    Eat regular meals and take your time. Drink about 8 cups of fluid a day, limit tea and coffee to 3 cups, cut down on alcohol and fizzy drinks, and limit fresh fruit to 3 portions a day.[NICE]

    Read more ↗

    4. Keep elimination short

    Follow strict low FODMAP for 2–6 weeks only. It's a test, not a forever diet.[Monash]

    Read more ↗

    5. Swap, don't drop

    Replace high-FODMAP foods with low-FODMAP foods from the same food group so your diet stays balanced.[Monash]

    Read more ↗

    6. Portion size matters

    Many foods are fine in small serves. Ask your dietitian about serving sizes, or use a lab-tested food guide such as the Monash University FODMAP app.[Monash]

    Read more ↗

    7. Test one group at a time

    Use a 3-day challenge with increasing serves, then a few days' break before the next group.[Monash]

    Read more ↗

    8. Aim for the least restriction

    Bring back every food you tolerate. It isn't meant to be a lifetime diet, and strict restriction can lower beneficial gut bacteria (bifidobacteria). The long-term effects of that aren't known yet.[Monash] [Staudacher 2012] [BSG]

    Read more ↗

    9. Fiber: soluble, slowly

    Soluble fiber such as psyllium (ispaghula) helps. Avoid insoluble fiber like wheat bran, which may make symptoms worse. Start at 3–4 g a day and build up slowly to avoid bloating.[BSG] [ACG] [NIDDK]

    Read more ↗

    10. "Gluten-free" isn't the goal

    A gluten-free diet isn't recommended for IBS. Any benefit people notice may come mainly from eating less fructan, a FODMAP in wheat, rather than from removing gluten.[BSG] [CAG]

    Read more ↗

    13. Skip IgG "intolerance" tests

    Elimination diets based on IgG antibody food tests aren't recommended for IBS (strong recommendation).[BSG]

    Read more ↗

    11. Probiotics: not routinely

    The ACG guideline (2021) suggests against probiotics for overall IBS symptoms, because the evidence is inconsistent. NICE (2008) says that if you choose to try one, take it for at least 4 weeks and watch the effect.[ACG] [NICE]

    Read more ↗

    12. Mind and gut

    Stress affects IBS. Gut-directed psychological therapies are recommended alongside diet.[ACG] If food rules start to cause anxiety, stop and speak to your clinician.

    Read more ↗
