    Ordering tips that work almost anywhere

    • Choose plain grilled, roasted or steamed meat, fish, eggs or tofu, with rice, potato or polenta.
    • Ask for sauces, dressings and gravies on the side. They're the most common hidden source of garlic and onion.
    • Ask whether the kitchen can cook your dish in plain oil or butter, without garlic or onion.
    • Hard cheeses such as parmesan and cheddar are low in lactose. Cream sauces and soft cheeses aren't.
    • This checker is a guide only. Recipes vary, so ask staff when in doubt.

    Learn

    This is education, not medical advice. IBS should be diagnosed by a clinician, and guidelines recommend doing this diet with a FODMAP-trained registered dietitian. Talk to your doctor before you start, especially if you are pregnant, have diabetes, are underweight, or have a history of disordered eating.

    Your journey at a glance

    The guide, step by step

    1Before you start: get checked

    The low FODMAP diet is meant for people who already have an IBS diagnosis. Other conditions can cause the same symptoms, so a clinician should rule them out first.[NIDDK] [NICE]

    See a doctor promptly, before any diet change, if you have:

    • Blood in your stool or rectal bleeding
    • Weight loss you can't explain
    • A family history of bowel, ovarian or celiac disease
    • Anemia, fever, or symptoms that wake you at night
    • Symptoms that started after age 50, or a sudden change in bowel habit

    These are "red flag" or "alarm" features listed in NICE and ACG guidance. They call for medical investigation, not a diet experiment.[NICE] [ACG]

    Get tested for celiac disease before cutting wheat

    The low FODMAP diet reduces wheat, rye and barley. Celiac blood tests and biopsies depend on you eating gluten, so going low-gluten first can give a false negative. The ACG guideline recommends celiac testing in people with IBS-type diarrhea symptoms.[ACG] [Celiac]

    Line up support

    • A FODMAP-trained dietitian. The British Society of Gastroenterology says the diet should be supervised by a trained dietitian.[BSG]
    • The Monash University FODMAP Diet app. It has lab-tested serving sizes for thousands of foods and is updated as foods are retested.[Monash]
    • A plan for the next 2–3 months. Avoid starting right before travel, holidays or major life events.

    Is this diet right for you?

    Guidelines rate the evidence for the low FODMAP diet as low quality but still recommend a limited trial, because it helps many people.[ACG] [BSG] In the UK, simpler first-line advice is often tried first: regular meals, less caffeine, alcohol and fizzy drinks, and gradual soluble fiber.[NICE] If that hasn't helped, the low FODMAP diet is the usual next step.

    2Understand what you're testing

    FODMAP stands for Fermentable Oligosaccharides, Disaccharides, Monosaccharides And Polyols. These are short-chain carbohydrates that are poorly absorbed in the small intestine. They draw water into the gut and are fermented by bacteria in the colon, which produces gas. In people with IBS, whose gut is often more sensitive, this can cause bloating, pain, diarrhea or constipation.[Monash]

    The diet tests six groups one at a time. Most people react to only some of them; in a University of Michigan study, patients reacted to an average of two groups.[Michigan]

    GroupCommon high sourcesTypical lower-FODMAP swaps
    Fructose monosaccharideHoney, mango, apples, pears, agave, high-fructose corn syrupOranges, strawberries, blueberries, kiwi, maple syrup
    Lactose disaccharideMilk, yogurt, ice cream, soft cheeses like ricotta and cottage cheeseLactose-free milk and yogurt, hard cheeses like cheddar and parmesan
    Fructans oligosaccharideWheat, rye and barley breads and pasta, onion, garlic, inulin or chicory rootRice, oats, quinoa, spelt sourdough, garlic-infused oil, chives, green tops of spring onion
    GOS oligosaccharideChickpeas, lentils, kidney beans, cashews, pistachiosSmall serves of canned, rinsed lentils or chickpeas, firm tofu, peanuts, walnuts
    Sorbitol polyolStone fruit (apricot, peach, plum), blackberries, sugar-free gum and mints (E420)Grapes, pineapple, citrus; check labels for "-itol" sweeteners

    Portion size matters. Many foods are low FODMAP in a small serve and high in a large one. Monash retests foods regularly and updated many serving sizes in 2025, so use the app for exact amounts rather than any fixed list, including this one.[Monash] [Monash]

    Foods that are naturally free of FODMAPs include plain meat, fish, eggs, firm tofu, most oils, rice, oats, potatoes, carrots, spinach, tomatoes and cucumber.

    3Track a baseline week

    Before changing anything, record what you eat and how you feel for about a week. NIDDK suggests writing down what you eat and drink each day, along with your symptoms and when they happen, and sharing that with your doctor.[NIDDK] This baseline lets you tell later whether the diet actually helped.

    How to score

    Rate each symptom from 0 (none) to 10 (worst imaginable) once a day, at the same time. Note stool form too: the Bristol Stool Form Scale runs from type 1 (hard lumps) to type 7 (watery); types 3–4 are typical.

    Also log things that affect IBS but aren't food: stress, sleep, your menstrual cycle, exercise, and medications.

    Use the Log tab. Entries are tagged with your current phase automatically.

    4Phase 1: Low FODMAP (2–6 weeks)

    Swap high-FODMAP foods for low-FODMAP alternatives in the same food group, for example an orange instead of an apple. The aim is substitution, not cutting out whole food groups, so your diet stays nutritious.[Monash] Monash recommends 2 to 6 weeks for this phase.

    Do

    • Plan meals and shop with a list for the first week
    • Check labels for honey, inulin, chicory root, high-fructose corn syrup, onion and garlic powder, and sweeteners ending in "-itol"
    • Use garlic-infused oil: fructans don't dissolve in oil, so it carries the flavor without the FODMAPs
    • Spread fruit out: about one serve per meal or snack
    • Keep up fiber from oats, potatoes with skin, kiwi, chia, linseed and allowed vegetables
    • Keep logging symptoms daily

    Avoid

    • Staying in this phase longer than needed. Long-term restriction can reduce beneficial gut bacteria and makes eating harder than it needs to be.[ACG]
    • Cutting out foods that aren't high in FODMAPs "just in case"
    • Expecting perfection. A slip-up isn't a failure; note it and carry on.

    A sample low FODMAP day

    • Breakfast: Oats with lactose-free milk, blueberries and a teaspoon of maple syrup
    • Lunch: Rice or quinoa bowl with chicken or firm tofu, spinach, carrot, cucumber and an olive oil and lemon dressing
    • Snack: An orange and a small handful of walnuts
    • Dinner: Salmon with potatoes, green beans and zucchini cooked in garlic-infused oil
    5Check-in: did it help?

    Compare your recent diary scores with your baseline. The app compares them for you on the Today screen when you reach the check-in.

    Symptoms clearly better

    FODMAPs are likely playing a role. Move on to Phase 2 to find out which ones. Don't stay on the strict diet: reintroduction is where you learn what you can eat.

    Little or no change after 4–6 weeks

    FODMAPs may not be your main trigger. Return to your normal diet and talk to your doctor or dietitian about other options. Guidelines also support soluble fiber such as psyllium, medicines targeted to your IBS type, and gut-directed psychological therapies such as CBT or gut-directed hypnotherapy.[ACG] [BSG]

    6Phase 2: Reintroduce one group at a time

    Stay on your low FODMAP base diet while you test one FODMAP group at a time, using a food that mainly contains that one FODMAP, such as mango for fructose.[Monash] Monash estimates this phase takes 8 to 12 weeks.[Monash]

    The 3-day challenge

    1. Day 1: Eat a moderate serve of the test food.
    2. Day 2: If you feel fine, eat a larger serve.
    3. Day 3: If still fine, eat a large serve, about what you'd normally eat.
    4. Then a washout: Return to strict low FODMAP for 2–3 days, or until symptoms settle, before the next test.

    If you get moderate or severe symptoms on any day, stop that challenge. Don't move to the next day. Wait until you're back to baseline before testing the next group.[Monash]

    Fructans are often tested separately for grains, garlic and onion, because people react differently to each. Monash revised its recommended challenge foods and serving sizes in 2025, so get the exact foods and amounts for each day from the Monash app or your dietitian.[Monash]

    Tips for a clean test

    • Test when life is relatively calm. Stress, illness, poor sleep or your period can cause symptoms that have nothing to do with the food.
    • Don't combine test foods with other high-FODMAP foods.
    • If a result is unclear, repeat that challenge later.
    • A reaction is about amount, not all-or-nothing. Note the largest serve you tolerated.

    The app walks you through each challenge day by day on the Today screen.

    7Phase 3: Your long-term diet

    The goal of the whole process is the least restrictive diet that keeps your symptoms under control. You only limit the groups, and amounts, that you reacted to, and bring everything else back.[Monash] [BSG]

    • Tolerated groups: Eat these freely again.
    • Partly tolerated: Eat them in the amounts that were fine for you, and spread them out over the day.
    • Not tolerated: Limit them for now, but retest every few months. Tolerance often changes over time.

    Your personal results are on the Today screen once you reach this phase.

    Other habits that help

    • Eat regular meals and don't rush them. Several smaller meals may help if large meals bring on cramping.[NIDDK]
    • Increase soluble fiber, such as psyllium, gradually. Insoluble bran doesn't seem to help.[ACG]
    • Notice how caffeine, alcohol, fatty meals and fizzy drinks affect you.[NICE]
    • Manage stress, sleep and activity. The gut-brain connection is a real part of IBS, and gut-directed psychological therapies are recommended in guidelines.[ACG]

    Diet guidelines at a glance

    The key rules from the guides above in one place, each with its source. Numbers in brackets link to the full reference at the bottom of the page.

    1. Diagnosis first

    Get an IBS diagnosis and celiac testing while still eating gluten, and check for red flag symptoms before changing your diet.[ACG] [NICE] [Celiac]

    2. Use expert help

    The low FODMAP diet should be supervised by a trained dietitian, and reintroduction should follow your own tolerance.[BSG]

    3. Start with the basics

    Eat regular meals and take your time. Drink about 8 cups of fluid a day, limit tea and coffee to 3 cups, cut down on alcohol and fizzy drinks, and limit fresh fruit to 3 portions a day.[NICE]

    4. Keep elimination short

    Follow strict low FODMAP for 2–6 weeks only. It's a test, not a forever diet.[Monash]

    5. Swap, don't drop

    Replace high-FODMAP foods with low-FODMAP foods from the same food group so your diet stays balanced.[Monash]

    6. Portion size matters

    Many foods are fine in small serves. Check lab-tested serving sizes in the Monash app.[Monash]

    7. Test one group at a time

    Use a 3-day challenge with increasing serves, then a washout before the next group.[Monash]

    8. Aim for the least restriction

    Bring back every food you tolerate. Long-term strict restriction risks poor nutrition and changes to gut bacteria.[ACG] [BSG]

    9. Fiber: soluble, slowly

    Soluble fiber such as psyllium helps IBS symptoms, while wheat bran doesn't. Increase fiber gradually to avoid gas.[ACG] [NIDDK]

    10. "Gluten-free" isn't the goal

    A gluten-free diet isn't recommended for IBS on its own. Wheat matters here because of its fructans, not its gluten.[BSG]

    11. Probiotics: not routinely

    The ACG guideline suggests against probiotics for overall IBS symptoms, because the evidence is inconsistent.[ACG]

    12. Mind and gut

    Stress affects IBS. Gut-directed psychological therapies are recommended alongside diet.[ACG] [BSG] If food rules start to cause anxiety, stop and speak to your clinician.
